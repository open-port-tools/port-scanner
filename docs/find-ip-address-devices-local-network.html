Whether you plugged in a headless Raspberry Pi, installed a network printer, or want to check for unknown devices on your Wi-Fi, scanning your local subnet reveals active IP addresses without needing to log into your router.

Why ARP & Ping Alone Often Miss Devices

Standard tools like arp -a only display devices your computer has recently communicated with, and Windows/macOS firewalls frequently block ICMP ping requests by default. Checking common TCP ports (like 22, 80, 443, 445, and 8080) across the entire /24 subnet reliably discovers active hardware.

Step-by-Step: Discover Network Devices in Seconds

  1. Download and open Open Port Scanner.
  2. Click the LAN subnet button (the tool automatically ignores loopback and VPN tunnels to locate your physical adapter's subnet).
  3. Keep the Quick (25 ports) preset selected and click Scan.
  4. Review the results table:
    • SSH banners (Port 22): Usually indicate Linux servers, Raspberry Pis, or NAS devices.
    • HTTP titles (Port 80 / 443 / 8080): Immediately name routers, printers (e.g., HP LaserJet), Proxmox nodes, and Home Assistant panels.
    • SMB (Port 445) or RDP (Port 3389): Identify Windows workstations and shared file servers.

Command-Line Discovery

# Discover all active hosts on your current network:
port-scanner --lan --profile quick

# Export the inventory to CSV for record-keeping:
port-scanner --lan --profile quick -o office-devices.csv
